# Fraud Investigation Workflow

**Status:** STABLE (mechanism) — investigation *process* detail (what an analyst actually does step by step) is thinner than the alert-generation mechanism.

## Trigger

An alert surfaces on `GET /admin/risk/alerts` — generated by the CatBoost fraud model, Isolation Forest anomaly detector, or a velocity-rule breach flagged for review rather than auto-declined.

## Actors

`fraud_analyst` role.

## What the analyst sees

Per the alert response schema: `user_id`, `alert_type`, `severity`, `fraud_score`, `evidence`, and a `recommended_action` — meaning the system is designed to hand the analyst a starting recommendation, not just raw signals, reducing investigation time.

## Steps (as far as documented)

1. Analyst reviews the alert and its evidence.
2. Analyst either dismisses (false positive, no action), escalates (needs further review or a different role's input), or confirms (real fraud).
3. On confirmation: `POST /admin/risk/blacklist` adds the relevant entity (CNIC/phone/device/IP/merchant), which feeds back into Layer 1 for all future checks.

## Known gap

**No endpoint exists to remove a blacklist entry** (`GW-GAP-07`) — an investigation that concludes a prior blacklist action was a false positive currently has no system-supported way to reverse it. This is a real operational risk: a wrongly blacklisted customer has no path back to `active` eligibility short of a direct database change, which itself bypasses the audit trail this whole process is meant to maintain.

## What's not documented

- Case-management detail: is there a formal "case" record beyond the alert itself, or does investigation happen ad hoc against the alert and blacklist entry alone?
- Escalation path for ambiguous cases (does the analyst have a second-level reviewer, or is the decision final at this role?).
- Coordination with law enforcement/FMU for confirmed serious fraud — connects to the broader AML/STR gap in [`../11-compliance/37-kyc-aml-policy.md`](../11-compliance/37-kyc-aml-policy.md).
