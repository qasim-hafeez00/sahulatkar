# SECP — Compliance Matrix

Covers: `docs/compliance/01-SECP/` (22 documents) and `docs/compliance/12-MODARABA/` (4 documents). See `00-MASTER-INDEX.md` for the Track A/B/C applicability legend.

**SECP's overall regulatory posture toward SahulatKar's business model:** SECP's digital-lending framework is built entirely around the assumption that a BNPL app is operated by, or under the direct API-integrated umbrella of, a **licensed lending NBFC** — there is no third path. The NBFC Regulations 2008 (as amended by SRO 2120(I)/2025) formally defines BNPL and Digital Lending, sets category-specific capital minimums as low as Rs 20–40 million for a pure digital-lending license, and explicitly provides an **"Embedded Lending"** mechanism (Circular 12/2024 §2(c)) letting a licensed NBFC extend services via API integration with a whitelisted third-party platform — this is the specific regulatory hook Track B (SahulatKar as technology provider) would use. Separately, SECP's P2P Service Provider framework (Reg. 35D) explicitly extends to "BNPL and working capital finance," a third structural option not previously flagged: a marketplace model where SahulatKar operates as an intermediary platform rather than the lender of record.

**Important supersession chain identified during this review:** Circular 12 of 2024 explicitly **supersedes** Circular 15 of 2022, Circular 10 of 2023, and Circular 15 of 2023 (the original pricing-caps circular). The older circulars are retained in this library for historical/interpretive context (SECP's collection-conduct and disclosure language evolved from them with only minor changes), but **Circular 12 of 2024 is the current authoritative text** and its numbers should be treated as controlling wherever they differ from the superseded circulars.

## Shariah Governance Regulations 2023, Guidelines for Offering Islamic Financial Services 2023, Shariah-Compliant Financing Guidebook 2026, Report on Shariah-Compliant Digital Financing, Diagnostic Review of Islamic Finance in the NBFI Sector

*These five documents are the single most operationally important cluster in the entire SECP library for SahulatKar specifically — they define what makes a BNPL structure Shariah-compliant under SECP's own framework, as distinct from SBP's banking-sector Shariah Governance Framework (already extracted in detail in `02-SBP-matrix.md`, including the ownership/possession sequencing, price-lock, and late-fee-as-charity rules that closely track what SahulatKar's `contract_generator.py`/`charity_service.py`/`late_fee_service.py` already appear to implement). **These five documents have not yet had a dedicated read-through in this session** — they were prioritized for collection but the deep-read pass ran out of time/budget. Given the SBP Shariah material already extracted maps very closely onto the same underlying AAOIFI-derived principles (Murabaha possession/qabd, fixed pricing, charity-routed penalties), it is a reasonable working assumption that SECP's NBFC-specific framework imposes materially similar requirements — but this must be **confirmed by an actual read of these SECP-specific documents**, not assumed, before being treated as authoritative for a lawyer/Shariah-advisor review. This is the single highest-priority item for the next research pass.*

## Companies Regulations 2024, Fintech Framework for the Non-Banking Sector, Regulatory Sandbox Guidelines 2019, Prudential Regulations for NBFCs, Prudential Regulations for Consumer Financing & NBFCs, Credit Rating Companies Regulations

*These six documents have not yet had a dedicated read-through in this session. Based on their titles and SECP's general regulatory pattern: Companies Regulations 2024 governs corporate administration (filings, meetings, registers) rather than product-level BNPL conduct — lower priority for this matrix.
