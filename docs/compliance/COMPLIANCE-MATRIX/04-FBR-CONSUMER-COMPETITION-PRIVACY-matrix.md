## Data Privacy — Personal Data Protection Bill (draft, not yet enacted)

**Status: this is a draft bill, not current law.** Pakistan's closest enforceable data-protection provisions today are PECA 2016 (already covered in `03-PAKISTAN-LAW-NADRA-PTA-matrix.md` — unauthorized access/disclosure offences) — there is no comprehensive data-protection statute in force. The rows below describe what the draft *would* require if enacted, recommended as a forward-looking design baseline given the volume of NADRA/biometric/financial data SahulatKar handles, not as a current binding legal obligation.

| Requirement (clause/section cited) | Business Rule for SahulatKar (design baseline, not yet legally mandatory) | Workflow / System | Applicability | Disclosure to customer? | Audit evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| §13 — personal data breach must be notified to the Commission and affected data subject "without undue delay," target **within 72 hours** of the controller becoming aware (later notification permitted with a stated valid reason); notification must include breach nature, scope, contact point, likely consequences, and mitigation measures taken | Design SahulatKar's incident-response process to this 72-hour standard now, before it's legally mandatory — it's a reasonable, internationally-consistent bar (mirrors GDPR's 72-hour rule) and retrofitting an incident-response SLA under actual regulatory pressure later is much harder than designing for it now | gateway admin_compliance.py incident-response workflow; notification-service breach-notification templates | Applicable (design baseline — draft law, not current legal obligation) | Yes, if enacted or adopted as internal policy | Incident response runbook targeting 72-hour notification; breach register (§13(4) requires a maintained record of all breaches regardless of notification outcome) |
| §31–32 — personal data (other than "critical personal data") may be transferred abroad only where the destination offers adequate protection or specific conditions are met (binding contract, explicit consent, international cooperation obligation); **"critical personal data" shall only be processed on servers/infrastructure located within Pakistan** | If SahulatKar (or its vendors — cloud hosting, SMS aggregator, analytics) processes any data offshore, confirm none of it would qualify as "critical personal data" under this draft's eventual final categorization, and build in data-localization as a default architecture choice rather than an exception | Infrastructure/hosting architecture decisions (cloud region selection); vendor contracts requiring in-country processing where applicable | Applicable (design baseline) | No | Data-residency architecture documentation; vendor contract clauses on data location |
| §6–7 — consent must be obtained for personal data processing, with clear notice to the data subject of purpose, scope, and rights | SahulatKar's KYC/onboarding consent flow (already needed for NADRA verification consent, per the NADRA matrix rows) should be designed to also satisfy this broader consent-and-notice standard, not just NADRA's narrower requirement | credit-engine/gateway onboarding consent capture | Applicable (design baseline) | Yes | Consent capture UI/flow documentation; notice content shown at consent point |
| §16–22 — data subject rights to access, correct, and (§23) withdraw consent for their personal data | Build a self-service or support-mediated channel for customers to request access to, correction of, or consent-withdrawal for their stored personal data — this exceeds what PECA or the current credit-bureau-specific dispute rights (already covered in `02-SBP-matrix.md`) provide, since it would cover all personal data, not just bureau-reported credit data | gateway customer support/data-request handling | Applicable (design baseline) | Yes | Data-subject-request intake and fulfillment log |
